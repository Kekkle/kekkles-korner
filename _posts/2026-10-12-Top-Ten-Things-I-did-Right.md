---
title: "Top Ten Things I did Right for My Hyrox Solo Race"
date: 2026-10-12
---

In October I completed my first Hyrox solo race and finished in 1:47 minutes–a time I was not immediately impressed by. So yes, I will most likely be racing again in order to see if I can improve on it.

But before I get into all the things I could have done better or am looking to improve, here are the top ten things I did right prior to and on race day:

### 5 things I did right prior to race day

1. **Began Hyrox training well in advance.** In April I committed to racing a Hyrox in October and put together a month by month training plan in order to be race-ready.
2. **Focused on running.** I read all the posts about how Hyrox is 50% running so I focused on improving my running base. I built my running up slowly–increasing my weekly 3km and 5km runs by 500-800m at a time, until my weekly 3km became a 5km and my weekly 5km became a weekly 7-8km. My training program included three running days (1 compromised run, 1 long run, 1 sprint or 1 temp run).
3. **Trained compromised running.** I again read all the posts about the difference between running a 1km on fresh vs tired legs, the latter being what Hyrox demands, so at about 4 months prior to race date, I added a compromised run to my weekly program.
4. **Reduced weight training sessions.** Previously I had been training 4x per week with weights. I learned from [DocLyssFitness](https://doclyssfitness.com/) that with hybrid training something has to give in order to make room for the weaker sport. As running was my weaker skill, I reduced my weight training days to 3x p/week in the first two months, and then eventually to 2x per week (total body), in order to provide enough time for run days plus recovery.
5. **Trained stations with heavier weights (except wall balls!).** Instead of 10kg lunges, I did 20kg. Instead of a 105kg sled, I pushed 130kg. I initially started training with 6kg wall balls, but learned from a coach that this can throw off one's aim and balance when using the regular 4kg weight. So wall balls I trained with race weight (4kg). I added 100 wall balls to the end of every leg day workout, and in the end, this method meant everything felt manageable on race day.

### 5 things I did right on race day

6. **Nutrition.** I nailed my nutrition (and hydration) the week before, the day before, and the day of race day. And for this, I am so proud of myself. Along with hydration. The week prior I served just a tad more carbs to my meals (eg., 1.5 vs 1 cup rice). The day before race day, I intentionally ate lower fibre foods, normal protein (1.6-2g p/kg), and added extra carbs to each meal. I drank fluids heavily throughout the day, as well as one electrolyte pack. On the day of the race, I had my usual breakfast of eggs and toast, a mid morning snack of a banana, a rice-rich lunch with some chicken breast, and freshly squeezed orange juice and toast 2 hours before race time. I drank one more stick of electrolytes in the morning. One hour before race time, I ate a carb jelly stick, and before the row station I had a 30g carb liquid. By the end of the race, I felt full of energy and like I had more to give.
7. **I arrived early.** Arriving 1.5 hours early gave me time to check in, examine the arena, spectate the current athletes, have a feel for the area, warm up and be ready at the start tunnel on time for my wave.
8. **Visualized before race day.** In the week prior to, the night before, and the morning of, I visualized running through every station, with different outcomes and potential worst case scenarios. My brain had familiarity and a plan going into the race.
9. **Stuck to my pacing goals.** I was intent on going slow on the first run and the ski erg (thinking 6/10 effort), which I stuck with although those around me kept a much faster pace. I maintained a slow and steady pace in the first half, and gave a little more effort after the rowing station.
10. **Wore my kit!**--Even though I felt a bit self conscious at the start in my bright orange shorts and blue sports bra, I reminded myself that this race was for me (and my daughter) and no one would give a flying hoot anyway. Afterwards I realized how advantageous my bright shorts were because my family could spot me easily wherever was I running (and later I could easily locate myself in the back of other's photos and videos on Insta :)).

All in all, it was a fun, high-energy experience that I so enjoyed taking part in. In my next post, I’ll go over some of the things that came up during the race that I did not expect or prepare for, as well as what I would do differently next time.
