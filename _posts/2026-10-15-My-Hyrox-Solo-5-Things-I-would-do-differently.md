---
title: "My Hyrox Solo: 5 Things I Would Do Differently"
date: 2026-10-15
---

I had two main goals for myself going into my first Hyrox solo:

1. Make it through the whole race without quitting (whether due to fatigue, injury/cramps, or negative self talk), and
2. Use this race to gather data (how do I feel at each station, where are my weak points) and afterwards I could decide whether I would want to race it again and if so what I would want to work on next time.

Seeing as I am interested in a repeat, here are the five things I would do differently:

1. **I would train running for redundancy.** The aim would be to get my brain used to repetition. Training on the treadmill watching meters turn into km is not the same as running loops–so I would include outdoor “loop-runs” in my week.
2. **I would train to improve my running cadence.** During training, specifically when using a treadmill, my cadence was pretty okay–at around 180bpm. However, as I don’t have a fancy watch/fitness tracker, when I train outdoors I’ve been unable to really keep track of this and I wasn’t aware that most likely my cadence is not the right tempo during those runs. So, on the track during race day I ran the same style/pace/cadence as my outdoor runs. Later when I looked at videos my husband filmed of me, my cadence was so slow that I think this was part of the reason for my knee pain–my feet were just too long on the ground.
3. **I would train the sled push with a sandbag in front of the sled–like I had seen recommended on Insta.** While I used a much heavier weight in training, the sled push surprised me by how shitty the carpet traction was. Every push tilted the sled forward. Training with a bag/hindrance in front of the sled may have helped to simulate the accurate feel of the carpet on race day.
4. **Additionally, I would position my body differently at the sled push.** I thought I was keeping good form when pushing, but in my photos I could see that I had actually not been as low as I should have been. I had my chin resting on the center pole–which worked in training because the pole was much lower at my gym sled!--but the pole was higher at Hyrox and so my upper body was less horizontal. Next time I should position my head to the side of the pole for better upper body positioning.
5. **I would go a bit harder during the second half of the race–specifically at the lunges and wall balls.** I kept a lot in the tank because I wasn’t sure just how much of a toll the entire race would be. Seeing that I had a good amount of energy left at the finish, if in my next race I'm feeling good at that row station, then I would give a bit more “welly” at every station after.

I have considered doing a doubles race next time (provided I can find a partner) but my one hesitation is the pacing. I imagine pacing with a partner is a lot more taxing (mentally and physically) than relying on your own mental and physical strength for feedback.

For the time being, I’ll focus on improving my running while I consider how and when to sign up for my next Hyrox.
